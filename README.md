Our perception of loudness is approximately logarithmic rather than linear.
This means that increasing the volume by the same numerical amount does not necessarily produce the same perceived increase in loudness.
Like, increasing a music volume from 2 to 12 can feel much more noticeable than 60 → 70, because our auditory system is more sensitive to 
relative changes in sound intensity than to absolute changes. This is why sound levels are commonly represented using 
the logarithmic decibel (dB) scale: a change in decibels represents a multiplicative change in physical sound intensity.



+10 dB → 10× the sound intensity
+20 dB → 100× the sound intensity
+30 dB → 1000× the sound intensity
